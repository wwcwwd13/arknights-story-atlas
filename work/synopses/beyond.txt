==No Sequel==
One morning, [[Hoshiguma]] decides to spend her day off riding her motorcycle. She asks a half-asleep [[Swire]] to help her choose her shoes, but the [[Feline]] explodes in frustration, having been dealing with the aftermath of the [[Chernobog]]-[[Lungmen]] crisis as well as the [[Rat King]], the responsibility falling on her after [[Ch'en]]'s resignation. Hoshiguma reminds the stressed Swire not to cry, much to her annoyance, and Swire expresses her intent to take over the [[L.G.D.]] now that Ch'en is gone. Swire also complains that Hoshiguma called for her despite knowing she had barely slept, having worked late the previous night, but Hoshiguma pacifies her by offering breakfast she had just bought. She had mistakenly bought two portions, causing Swire to remark on Ch'en's strange taste in food and recommend a better place to Hoshiguma. Swire then invites Hoshiguma to go window-shopping with her later, planning to buy a piece of branded jewelry that Hoshiguma recalls Ch'en had also been interested in, prompting Swire to add that Ch'en has a better eye for clothes and jewelry.

After declining Swire's invitation, Hoshiguma heads out and greets several officers, promising to treat them to a feast once they finish handling the massive backlog of work. The conversation turns to [[Rhodes Island]], with Hoshiguma advising them not to stir up trouble with the pharmaceutical company, while some officers admit to having drunk with its members and a [[Laterano]] monastery girl. After her ride, Hoshiguma comments that the exhaust of her motorcycle feels strange but reflects that both she and the machine are still far from growing old. She muses whether Ch'en has gotten into any fights at Rhodes Island when [[Shirayuki]] suddenly appears, having been assigned to protect Hoshiguma. Shirayuki reports that the people at Rhodes Island are boisterous and full of energy, and that Ch'en is doing well. Hoshiguma reassures her that she is not worried about Ch'en, though she reflects that the chivalry Ch'en upholds is not worth the scars it leaves, and fears that Ch'en cannot let go of her grief. When Hoshiguma denies being a chivalrous person herself, Shirayuki offers her the latest issue of a comic before leaving. Amused, Hoshiguma finds that it is the artist's debut work and that the heroine, who wields a giant shield, bears an uncanny resemblance to her.

Hoshiguma continues her ride when someone suddenly speeds past her, reminding her of the rumored "Ghost Driver." She soon recognizes the vehicle as belonging to Fumizuki. The two exchange greetings and compliments about each other's motorcycles. During their conversation, Hoshiguma mentions that the Stream-35LL Luminous Luxury parked downstairs from Wei Yenwu's residence for the past two days definitely isn't his, noting that he doesn't seem like the type to enjoy biking.

==Boundless Wilds==
The story focuses on the aimless journey of [[Chiave's Gang]] in the [[Siracusa|Siracusan]] wilderness, as they can no longer return to Vite after defying ''Famiglia'' Trin. Along the way, the car suddenly breaks down, forcing them to halt for repairs. [[Chiave]] watches bugs while [[Aosta]] and [[Broca]] attempt to fix it. Afterwards, the two go get Chiave, who makes crude jokes about pissing in the barrens. They continue the ride with Aosta driving, and he explains to Chiave their plan to head toward the nearby Locomotiva City. They first chat about the long-disbanded Sicilians, and Chiave declares the three of them to be members of the new Chiave ''Famiglia'', calling Aosta the ''consigliere'' and Broca the underboss, much to their amusement.

Along the ride, Aosta rejects Chiave's request to eat their food supplies, and Broca offers a novel about a pair of lovers torn apart by their ''famiglie'' to Chiave, prompting Chiave to ask the other two whether they have girlfriends. Unsurprisingly, they are all single. Aosta answers that he prefers historical and political titles, boring Chiave. Just then, the gang notices a caravan being robbed by bandits. Hoping that the caravan will reward them, they decide to help scare off the bandits, giving the merchants time to escape before Chiave and Broca jump back into the car. They repel the bandits chasing them, but after a while, Aosta proclaims that the bad news is their car is running out of fuel, while the good news is that they still have a chance to reach Locomotiva before it's exhausted. 

Chiave keeps coughing along the way until they arrive at Locomotiva. The three push the car for the final stretch, reminiscing about how they met one another. Aosta was sent to clean Chiave up for beating his family's mafiosos, but they ended up becoming friends after fighting, while Broca was sold out by his ''famiglia'' but later saved by the other two. Chiave then complains about how he hates [[Oripathy]], having contracted it due to poor precautions as a mechanist, as the disease caused Layton of ''Famiglia'' Trin to throw them out. Chiave then asks them their opinion of dying in the barrens, remarking that it might not be so bad, before falling silent, causing the other two to panic. Broca reassures Aosta that he has simply fallen asleep. A Rhodes Island operator then finds them and offers to help with their car problems.

Aboard Rhodes Island, [[Jaye]] serves them sashimi as he listens to their story and asks if that was when they joined Rhodes Island. Aosta explains that they simply traded the car for medicine, with Chiave remarking that he thought Rhodes Island was pretty lousy, but they later joined after the operator saved them again when Chiave offended a ''famiglia'' head. Jaye mentions getting some [[Lungmen]] wine while Chiave begins recounting the story of how they got into trouble in Locomotiva.

==Fairytale Everlasting==
[[Iris]], the inheritor of the legendary "[[Victoria/Branches#Castle of Dreams|Castle of Dreams]]," is on her way to search for Mabel Grimm, a [[Feline]] girl with brown hair and green eyes, who left the Castle of Dreams twenty years ago after leaving an old radio as a memento. [[Bena]] asks Iris why she is in such a hurry despite having just received her staff and despite the castle's unsorted affairs, to which Iris only claims she wants to fulfill the promise on time. The [[Victoria/Branches#Castle of Dreams|Cautus keeper]] joins their conversation, reminding Bena not to break the radio and wishing Iris good luck. Iris starts her search in the village of Tonnerre, where Mabel used to live, but learns from a passerby that the villagers have long since moved, and the place has since turned into an industrial region. Nevertheless, she bears the bitterness and searches for clues in nearby villages to no avail, asking door-to-door while her vehicle is being repaired.

She then witnesses a conversation between an [[Infected]] child and her parents, and later finds a Rhodes Island operator in the village. They start off on the wrong foot, with Iris commenting on the operator's shifty appearance, and then she explains the details she knows about Mabel. The villager pressures the operator into helping her out, so to Iris's annoyance, he follows her around while she continues her search. Just then, the two hear the sound of an argument and find a couple arguing over their Infected child, who is the same child that Iris saw before. Iris finds the girl, named Dora, and warns her not to scratch herself, and the two then discuss fairy tales and the rumored Castle of Dreams. To Iris's surprise, the girl is distraught that she might have to leave behind her dolly and insists on protecting it forever. Iris tries to comfort her by offering to play jigsaws or bring her a new ragdoll, becoming apologetic as she realizes she cannot grant Dora's wishes nor heal her disease. Dora confesses that her friend Maemae had gone to the Castle of Dreams and had lots of fun, but Dora does not want the fairy to be saddened by the fact that she cannot grant all wishes.

Upon leaving Dora's home, Iris learns from the Rhodes Island operator that he had talked with Dora's parents and introduced them to a place where they could entrust their daughter for treatment. He tells her that stories and fairy tales provide people with hope, which is essential for those in terrible circumstances. After some time, he returns to the topic of Mabel, commenting that he knows someone who matches her description. Her search renewed by his words, she demands to know her current location, turning confused when he says she might be too late. He adds that the things that happened aren't his to tell, and that perhaps she should try to go meet her.

==A Streetcar Named Faith==
To solve the financial problems of Landen Monastery, [[Archetto|Hildegard]] leaves for Lungmen. She dreams of the monastery becoming a wine brewery before meeting [[Bison]], who informs her that her meeting with [[Eurill Pides]] will take place the next day and asks about the accommodation that MountainDash Logistics has provided for her. Hildegard insists on rejecting the reimbursement and explains that she will be taking a look around Lungmen. She then inquires how Bison can treat his father merely as his boss while working, her boldness surprising him. The two part on good terms, Hildegard seeing Bison's ambition to surpass his father and Bison finding her moderation admirable, and Hildegard agrees to share her stories of [[Laterano]] with Bison once she has spare time.

Hildegard lines up at a crowded dessert shop and leaves with overly sweet cakes after some time, coming across [[Snowsant]], who recognizes her as the Lateran visitor staying in the room across from Snowsant's temporary one. They introduce themselves, and Hildegard offers cakes to her, which the shy [[Liberi]] accepts. They spend the day together, discussing the lands they have visited. Snowsant describes Columbia as technological but somewhat inhuman, saying that her back-and-forth life in the research institute makes her feel queasy again, while Hildegard explains about Landen Monastery. It was a convent for training Apostolic warriors that accepted people from other countries, but it has lost all work to the [[Notarial Hall]] and received no grants from the Pope on the grounds that the monastery must learn to become self-reliant, leaving them teetering on the edge of bankruptcy. Hildegard coughs from her outburst, then adds that they still have their wheat fields. They reach the residence and part ways, with Snowsant telling Hildegard that she can come by any time.

On the day of the business meeting, Hildegard finally gets the chance to meet Eurill at a bar. She is impressed by his business strategies, and when she asks about him choosing Landen Monastery, he explains that it is difficult for a logistics company to expand beyond its country of registration. He expresses hope that their cooperation will open more bridges to connect people across [[Terra]], even if they must endure wars and Catastrophes to realize his project. Hildegard is stunned by his grand vision and asks whether Bison knows about it, to which Eurill replies that he does not, expressing trust in his son's yet-to-be-polished abilities. She admits that her own goal seems rather simplistic in comparison, and Eurill reassures her that cherishing one's homeland is not a self-serving pursuit.

After the meeting, Hildegard returns to her room, amazed yet unsettled by Eurill's words. She then hears an explosion and sees [[Penguin Logistics]] chasing a thief through the streets. Shortly after, Snowsant knocks on her door and enters, having accidentally burned her curtains the previous day and fallen into financial trouble. Watching the chase outside, Snowsant explains to Hildegard who the Penguin Logistics girls are, and the mention of [[Sora]] being an idol piques Hildegard's interest, prompting Snowsant to turn on the television so they can watch a music concert together. The next morning, Hildegard wakes to find Snowsant asleep and decides to take a walk around Lungmen, pondering the choices she can make to travel across Terra. Along the way, she encounters [[Emperor]], who claims to own the bar where she and Eurill had met. He encourages her to see the world and think bigger, remarking that she is easy on the eyes, just like Paul Landen, the monastery's founder who laid down his gun to take up a pen. Before Hildegard can respond, Emperor departs in a car driven by [[Texas]].

Before Hildegard's departure from Lungmen, she asks Eurill why he is always at the bar. He tells her that he bought it from an acquaintance and happens to like the ambiance, as well as the name "The Ends of the Earth." They toast to the project, and Hildegard muses that she wishes to see more of the world. She cites a fable called "La Bestia Superbia," in which an arrogant man slandered a Lateran mural because he believed it depicted a vile beast. She remarks that Eurill's goal of shattering arrogance between nations will be an arduous task and expresses her admiration. Relieved, Eurill asks her to one day take his son to Laterano so he can study further, as his mind has broadened somewhat after meeting [[Mostima]]. Hildegard finds the name familiar, noting that [[Sankta]] can live good lives no matter their situation due to their easygoing nature. Eurill then hands Hildegard a contact address for Rhodes Island, mentioning that Emperor wanted her to have it.

==Blind Journey==
[[Leonhardt]] and [[Ayerscarpe]] conduct a rescue mission in a [[Catastrophe]] warning area in [[Rim Billiton]] after receiving a distress signal, with [[Greyy]] accompanying them. They observe that a Catastrophe is expected to arrive within twenty hours. As Leonhardt drives the buggy, Greyy laments his lack of a driver's license, but the two Cautuses explain that standard protocol requires bringing a [[Catastrophe Messenger]] like Leonhardt when entering warning zones. Leonhardt adds that the distress signal was also encrypted in a Rim Billitonian manner. Greyy apologizes for insisting on taking the mission, but Ayerscarpe reassures him that it was Leonhardt who leapt into action first. When Greyy is thrown back from Leonhardt's rough driving, Ayerscarpe helps him up using the signal amplifier attached to his weapon, leading their conversation to shift toward the weapon itself. Leonhardt remarks that Catastrophe Messengers are often considered jinxes, and Ayerscarpe explains to Greyy that the news they bring tends to disrupt people's daily lives.

Along the way, their discussion shifts to the treatment of the [[Infected]] in Rim Billiton, where Leonhardt was still welcomed due to not having visible crystals, although people avoided shaking hands with him, and then to how the Infected are treated in the outside world, prompted by Greyy's curiosity. The two Cautuses comment that the way the Infected are portrayed by others is often erroneous, much like how someone familiar only with imitation firearms might question why a Lateran firearm can fire [[Originium]] bullets. Leonhardt then explains the difference between gun-shaped Arts Units and Lateran firearms, to which Ayerscarpe remarks that Leonhardt must have analyzed their structure with his [[Arts]]. Greyy sympathizes with his curiosity as a fellow caster, but Leonhardt cuts the topic short, fearing that Ayerscarpe might become too interested in Greyy's electrical Arts.

Greyy then admits that he wishes to help more people at Rhodes Island so he can earn money for his parents despite the civil war in [[Bolívar]]. Leonhardt gestures for Ayerscarpe to cheer the [[Perro]] up, so the bodyguard tells him that what matters are his efforts, not the results, and that Greyy's convictions might be greater than his or Leonhardt's. Leonhardt adds that he became a Catastrophe Messenger simply because he enjoyed it, not out of duty. In response to Greyy's question, Leonhardt explains that there are also Messengers who take on Catastrophes, and they are often extremists willing to sacrifice some lives to save many more. He also mentions Contingency Contracts, which Rhodes Island occasionally accepts, but Ayerscarpe advises Greyy to seek opportunities to do what he truly wants rather than blindly taking on responsibilities. This prompts Leonhardt to bring up Greyy's work at Rhodes Island, recalling the time he fainted while handling the Doctor's paperwork. As Greyy tries to explain himself, the light in his eyes makes the two Cautuses realize that he is, indeed, a professional.

Leonhardt wonders about the efficiency of [[Closure]]'s power system, prompting Ayerscarpe to jokingly claim that the data logger Closure installed on the buggy has a built-in voice recorder. The dry joke makes Leonhardt recall a dream in which he visited the Iberian seaside, with Ayerscarpe [[Exodus from the Pale Sea/Synopsis|having learned a sword technique based on tracing circles and fighting a man in a robe]]. When they arrive at the source of the signal, they find nothing. Ayerscarpe realizes that Leonhardt had intentionally slowed down, using his Arts while driving to survey ahead. Leonhardt explains to Greyy that it might have been a trap meant to lure in looters, and hopes they wouldn't face the annoying [[Rusthammer|Rusthammers]]. Though slightly disappointed, Greyy listens as Leonhardt tells him that knowledge and combat skills are no different for operators, and that as long as he has the will, he can become a reliable one himself. His words leave Greyy in awe.

==Sword and Scale==
[[Ch'en]] is in a [[Columbia|Columbian]] city on a mission as a Rhodes Island operator when she stops an Infected fugitive fleeing from the police. He claims that he was framed, insisting he only spoke with the deceased foreigner but was suspected simply because he is Infected. Ch'en reassures him that the Columbian courts are fairer than those in other places, recalling in a conversation with [[Amiya]] that this was one of the reasons she chose to travel to Columbia, though Amiya feared Ch'en might end up disappointed. The fugitive rebukes her, arguing that no one would ever support an Infected in court. He then laments that the people he once knew are long gone, revealing that the Old Tom was lynched and killed.

Ch'en recalls another conversation in which she asked Amiya who has the right to determine another person's guilt, believing herself to possess both the power to punish evil and the resolve to sacrifice everything for the justice she seeks. Rosmontis answers in Amiya's place, telling Ch'en that no one can truly judge another person, though they can still act to stop injustice. [[Rosmontis]] adds that she will one day grow into someone capable of discerning whether a person is good or bad. Back in the present, the Infected mocks Ch'en, claiming that she is only able to work alongside the non-Infected because she still holds value. Ch'en responds that she will help him regardless. She reflects on how she once thought Rhodes Island's ideals were naive, yet has come to realize that she is no different from them. She wonders if she can only change what lies within the reach of her sword, recalling [[Wei Yenwu]]'s words and concluding that she has no regrets about choosing a different path from the one he set for her. Though she still lacks a definitive answer, she understands that some goals must continue to be pursued no matter how distant they seem, and finds solace in knowing that Rhodes Island walks the same path as she does.

Days later, Ch'en receives a new mission list for her team and also informs the clerk about a new member. The clerk tells her that there is a rumor surrounding the murder incident. The fugitive was eventually apprehended, but later on, the real murderer somehow surrendered themselves to the police, and the fugitive was released. The clerk whispers to Ch'en that she knows the behind-the-scenes secret of the incident through accounts from her relative working in the police force: the actual killer was caught by someone and left in front of the station with evidence. Ch'en remarks that she would rather the local police do their job so that vigilantes would not exist. Ch'en leaves with a masked Operator, who is actually the Infected man she helped. In her heart, she says to Amiya that she will continue on her path despite the difficulties and disappointments.

==For a Better Self==
[[Beanstalk]], [[Utage]], and [[Myrrh]] are collecting herbs for the Medical Department. Myrrh thanks Beanstalk and Utage for helping her, the latter insisting that they are now friends since Myrrh's name is on her contact list. Myrrh is flabbergasted by the strange lists she has, while Beanstalk asks more about them. Beanstalk states that one of her pets has found a spot they have yet to cover, but Utage realizes that it is near the border of [[Iberia]]. Beanstalk mentions some of the strange rumors she has heard about Iberia, such as barren landscapes and nomadic cities capable of changing colors and flying away. Myrrh then offers her account that there are numerous ghost towns filled with buildings inlaid with jewels and many gold statues, as well as invisible things praying at churches and people losing their sanity. 

Her story spooks Beanstalk, while Utage insists that it must be fake. Utage also recalls a festival in which the dead climb out of their graves and the people lock themselves in their houses. Looking at the far-fetched rumors, Utage surmises that their lack of realism comes from Iberia's isolationism. She asks if they should continue north, but Beanstalk replies that they can just call it a day, as they've gathered plenty. Myrrh agrees absentmindedly, and when asked by Utage, admits she is curious about what Iberia is really like.

The story then shifts to two children chasing each other around in an Iberian town, José and Antonio. Antonio accuses José of badmouthing him at school. Just then, the boys accidentally stumble upon [[Quintus|a robed man]], whom they recall must have come to give a sermon. José insists that his words about Antonio stealing change are a lie, as he was jealous that he did not get enough to eat despite having better grades. He begs the priest not to take Antonio away, while Antonio insists that he did steal the money, begging José to stop lying for him. The priest muses that lies are vile if not followed through, but white lies are what make them human. Antonio confesses that he stole the money to help José pay for tuition, and the two boys confess their actions. The priest helps them see their mistakes and says they need only overcome their predicaments and keep their faith. Upon hearing someone calling him for the sermon, the priest asks the two to recognize their mistakes before everyone and reminds them to believe in themselves.
